What I Bring to Patient Care

Careful assessment, clear communication, and attention to everything that affects a patient’s recovery.

Safe Handoffs and Discharges

My MPH and healthcare technology background shape the questions I ask at every shift: Does the patient understand the plan? What could make discharge or follow-up difficult? What does the next nurse need to know at handoff? At Activate Care, I built care-coordination tools into EHR workflows and trained more than 100 clinicians to use them.

Advocacy Through Escalation

During my pediatric emergency preceptorship, a mother told me her son’s sickle cell pain had gone undertreated on earlier visits. His pain scores stayed at 8 to 10 and his vitals were trending the wrong way. When an urgent message to my preceptor went unanswered for 30 minutes, I took my assessment to the charge nurse, and his pain was addressed within 45 minutes. Listening to the family and presenting objective data is how I advocate.

Language Access at the Bedside

I am fluent in Spanish, and as the son of immigrants I know how hard it is to navigate care in a system built around someone else’s language. When I explained a child’s care to a Spanish-speaking family in the pediatric emergency department, their questions came freely and their fear eased. In acute care, where clarity is safety, language access is a clinical intervention.

Kindness as Clinical Practice

Before nursing, I coached underserved youth through Athletic Scholars Academy, combining soccer with health education. That work taught me patience, consistency, and how to earn trust. Being nominated by faculty for the Ruth G. Franc Kindness Award reinforced my commitment to respectful, patient-centered care on every shift.
